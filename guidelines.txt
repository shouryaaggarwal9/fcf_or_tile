## Why it feels basic — the actual numbers in the engine

1. **Boards are tiny.** Hard cap is 48 tiles (`LATE_RECIPES`), and that only appears past level 30. Levels 1–30 are 12–30 tiles. An adult-focused stacker lives in the 80–150 tile range.
2. **Footprint is 4 columns wide** — a deliberate old constraint ("so a 320px screen keeps readable faces"). A 4-wide board means each upper tile covers at most 2–4 lower tiles, and because candidates are spread center-out, most cover only 1–2. Coverage barely binds; there are almost always many free picks.
3. **Depth is an illusion.** Recipes claim "6 layers," but tiles are spread evenly (`floor(tiles/layers)`), so upper layers are sparse. What creates difficulty in this genre is *density* — vertical stacks where one buried tile gates 3–4 above it — not the layer count itself.
4. **Kind math is generous.** With 48 tiles and 16 kinds, every kind appears exactly 3 times. Every triple is fully on the board from move one; the tray is never actually pressured. More kinds relative to tiles (and more copies of each kind buried across the stack) is the pressure dial.
5. **The aid economy is cozy by default.** Free safe hints, undo depth of 48, a free rescue per attempt, gentle level every 8th. Fine for comfort, but nothing demands focus.
6. **Objectives are slack.** Move limits give witness + max(5, 25%) picks — very forgiving.

**One critical hidden trap:** the generator verifies solvability by replaying a witness, but it never checks *peak tray occupancy* along the way. On today's small boards the peak never approaches 6, so it's never mattered. On 96–120 tile boards with 20 kinds, a random removal order can absolutely overflow the tray mid-replay — and `buildPuzzle` would throw "Generated level is not solvable" at runtime. Any size increase **must** add a seeded retry loop over removal orders until one keeps peak tray ≤ capacity−1. This is the single most important implementation detail.
